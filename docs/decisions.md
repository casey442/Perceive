# Design decisions — condensed log (2026-08-07)

Read this before proposing structural changes. Full versions live in the BoardPlanner Claude project under `decisions/`.

## Why the hero says "Keep the record." and shows no product
Tyler rejected a product-led v1 ("too product focused"). The homepage sells the buyer's world and routes; the product tour lives one click deeper. "Keep the record." comes from the buyer-language mine's headline candidates (institutional-memory angle: a 27-year board liaison's "my regret is that I did not keep a journal"). Search remains the wedge capability per confirmed rule R-4, but it leads the **How it works** page, not the homepage. AI never leads anywhere; it rides on search with page-level citations, because the researched objection to AI summaries in this exact market is "confident ignorance is corrosive."

## Why the audience is the board secretary
Confirmed rule R-1: zero verbatim software complaints exist from sitting board members anywhere; every reviewer and RFP author is the secretary / executive assistant / clerk. She is buyer, daily user, and pain-holder in one person. Board-member features are framed as findability that reduces calls to her desk. A hard directive sits on top: never imply board members are unprepared.

## Why proof is statutory deadlines, not testimonials
No customers yet. Fabricated quotes would breach rule R-7 (every claim traces to a live URL). Real statutory numbers (72-hour notice, Texas 3 business days, 48-hour packet policies) carry the stakes instead. Testimonial slot opens when real, named customers exist.

## Why the migration promise is in the hero metadata
The only confirmed K-12 RFP in this category (Topeka USD 501, 2025) exists to migrate off BoardDocs and states "historical data must be accessible through the new system (non-negotiable)." Migration anxiety is the #1 objection; it dies above the fold. Whether copy ever names BoardDocs explicitly is an open positioning/legal call.

## Palette history
v1 warm tan/beige/camel → rejected ("too tan"). v2 warm stone grey → superseded. v4 (final): achromatic black / silver / white, every grey a true R=G=B neutral. Standing constraints: no dark blue, no pink, light backgrounds with dark text.

## Type history
v1–v2 grotesque (Schibsted) → v3 (final): serif editorial — Fraunces display with italic accent words + Newsreader text. Bar to clear was Function Health's typographic confidence; the answer was character (civic-editorial serif) rather than imitation. Supporting devices: civic arch on hero media, ghost folio numerals, hairlines, small caps, paper grain, double-rule footer.

## Copy voice
All copy passes stop-slop: no em dashes, no filler adverbs, actors on every verb, no negative-listing or "not X but Y" rhetoric. Card copy uses the title as sentence subject ("The packet / Goes out days early."). One deliberate exception: "already" in the final CTA headline carries the urgency and stays. One CTA verb everywhere: "See a demo."

## Structural constraints (from Tyler's brief, standing)
Hero → 3-4 boxes → bottom band. Maximum two scrolls below the hero. ~350-word budget. One wink per page, at the exit ("Meeting adjourned.").
